1. **Democrats sweep U.S. Congress in midterms, sending gold above $4,000 and lifting bonds and renewables**  
   Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in a sweeping U.S. midterm election victory driven by voter frustration over inflation and concerns in exit polls regarding government corruption and billionaire influence. The outcome sent the two-year Treasury yield to a two-month low as Treasuries strengthened and the U.S. dollar fell against currencies including the British pound, while gold surged above $4,000 an ounce alongside broader gains in silver and precious metals. After an initial drop, S&P 500 futures turned positive as traders viewed the result and potential AI regulatory scrutiny as largely priced in, while European equities rallied, global renewable stocks jumped on expectations of more favorable industry policy, and Japanese stocks trimmed declines.

2. **China escalates stock market defense with state fund purchases and corporate buybacks**  
   China escalated its defense of the domestic stock market as state-backed funds increased equity purchases and more Chinese companies stepped up share buyback plans. The intervention follows recent meetings between regulators, banks, and brokerages to discuss liquidity support measures amid a softening forward earnings outlook and credit market strain.

3. **Meta targets up to $25B bond sale to fund AI expansion as debt costs rise**  
   Meta is seeking to raise between $20 billion and $25 billion in a bond sale as its debt costs rise and the company ramps up spending on infrastructure and AI models. The offering follows S&P Global Ratings' recent downward revision of Meta's outlook over free cash flow and capital expenditure concerns, and comes as major technology companies return to the U.S. bond market after a nearly three-month pause.

4. **AMD shares jump 8% as Q3 data center revenue nearly doubles on AI chip demand**  
   AMD reported better-than-expected third-quarter results as booming AI chip sales nearly doubled its data center revenue, sending its shares up 8%. The beat provides a counterweight to rising investor skepticism over AI spending that recently punished peer chipmakers.

5. **Marvell's acquisition of Lumentum valued at $115B enterprise value**  
   Following the initial announcement of its AI connectivity deal with Lumentum Holdings, Marvell's agreement to acquire Lumentum was disclosed at an enterprise value of $115 billion. The valuation makes the transaction one of the largest technology deals in history as Marvell seeks to capitalize on data center spending.
