# Market Watch — 2026-11-03

Updated: 2026-11-03T07:21:40.792848-05:00

1. **Nasdaq enters correction territory as Apple and Palantir slide after earnings**  
   Building on the multi-week technology selloff, U.S. tech stocks and the Nasdaq officially entered correction territory on Monday amid AI and U.S. midterm election risks. Equities were set for a lower open on Tuesday after Apple and Palantir shares slid in premarket trading following post-bell earnings reports, even as Palantir reported that its U.S. commercial revenue jumped 148% to $984 million to surpass its $953 million in government revenue for the first time.

2. **U.S. strikes Iran's Qeshm Island after crude oil plunges over 7% on peace talk hopes**  
   Following earlier reports that Washington would give diplomacy more time, crude oil prices plunged more than 7% on Monday on hopes for U.S.-Iran peace talks ahead of the U.S. midterm elections. However, the U.S. military carried out strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday even as Vice President Vance expressed hope for a negotiated settlement with Tehran and Strait of Hormuz talks kept oil markets on edge.

3. **PBOC raises FX reserve ratio to halt onshore yuan's 30-year record winning streak**  
   The People's Bank of China lifted its foreign-exchange reserve ratio, requiring banks to hold more foreign currencies in reserve to cool the yuan's rally. The intervention snapped the onshore yuan's longest winning streak in three decades.

4. **Reserve Bank of Australia hikes interest rates to 18-year high**  
   The Reserve Bank of Australia raised interest rates to an 18-year high to combat inflation, in line with economist expectations. The decision marks the central bank's fifth rate increase this year.

5. **Anthropic discloses $17B Q3 revenue and $895.4B in commitments as IPO investors push for $1.5T valuation**  
   Following its initial S-1 prospectus last week that only covered financials through the first half of the year, Anthropic filed a revised S-1 disclosing $17 billion in third-quarter revenue alongside surging costs and $895.4 billion in purchase commitments. Meanwhile, institutional investors planning to buy into the IPO are pushing for a more cautious valuation closer to $1.5 trillion following the broader selloff in technology stocks.
