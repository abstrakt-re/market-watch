# Market Watch — 2026-10-20

Updated: 2026-10-20T07:16:00.430151-04:00

1. **Paris stocks and French bonds slump as violent protests compound debt crisis fears**  
   Following the initial drop in the euro as French spending-cut protests broke out ahead of a budget vote, the unrest escalated into weekend riots involving more than 500,000 people and triggered a broader sell-off across Paris-listed stocks and French government bonds. With the French government in crisis mode, investors are increasingly concerned that political resistance to fiscal consolidation will deepen France's debt crisis.

2. **PBOC plans shift to overnight reverse repo policy rate that could lower borrowing costs**  
   China's central bank signaled that its overnight reverse repurchase rate will replace the current seven-day rate as the new policy benchmark, aligning its framework closer with global peers. The shift has the potential to push down borrowing costs as Beijing seeks to support sluggish domestic demand alongside strong tech export growth.

3. **SoftBank CDS hit four-year high and Microsoft exits Nscale deal amid AI funding strain**  
   Extending the AI credit jitters that battered neocloud and tech stocks last week, SoftBank Group Corp.'s credit-default swaps climbed to their highest level in nearly four years amid growing concerns over AI-related funding and a potential large acquisition. Separately, Microsoft pulled the plug on an Nscale data center deal as the debt-laden U.S. AI infrastructure build-out faces mounting scrutiny.

4. **Bank of Canada surveys show rising inflation expectations, lifting rate-hike bets**  
   Bank of Canada surveys showed rising inflation expectations and growing evidence that businesses plan to pass higher input costs on to customers. Following the survey release, more economists and traders boosted bets that the Bank of Canada will hike interest rates next week.

5. **Indonesian stocks rebound as exchange moves to cut tightly held shares after MSCI warning**  
   Reversing the multi-day rout that sent Indonesian equities to their lowest level since 2020, Indonesian stocks rebounded after authorities announced they will remove stocks with concentrated ownership from the benchmark index. The reform steps up Indonesia's response to MSCI Inc. following earlier market-manipulation fines as regulators work to avert a potential two-step market status downgrade.
