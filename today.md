# Market Watch — 2026-10-29

Updated: 2026-10-29T07:20:19.178849-04:00

1. **Federal Reserve holds rates steady with limited December guidance**  
   Following its September rate increase and hotter-than-expected September inflation data, the Federal Reserve kept interest rates unchanged as widely expected and offered limited guidance on a potential December hike. Stocks and 10-year Treasury yields edged higher while gold dipped below $4,000 an ounce as Wall Street read Chair Kevin Warsh as hesitant to hike aggressively.

2. **Alphabet and Samsung earnings drags snap tech stock rally**  
   Alphabet missed third-quarter earnings and revenue expectations—its first top- and bottom-line miss in nearly four years—sending its shares down 10% as it held 2026 capital expenditures unchanged while projecting a significant increase in 2027. Alongside a slide in Samsung Electronics shares despite record third-quarter profit, the results snapped this week's technology rally and revived investor concerns over heavy AI spending.

3. **Microsoft beats on 48% Azure growth while lower capex hits neocloud stocks**  
   Following recent voluntary buyout offers to 60,000 employees to balance AI infrastructure spending with capital efficiency, Microsoft reported better-than-expected quarterly earnings and revenue driven by 48% Azure growth, lifting its stock 4%. However, Microsoft's lower-than-expected capital expenditure figure signaled that infrastructure partners are taking longer to deliver capacity, sending shares of neocloud providers CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN lower.

4. **Vice President Vance engages Iran to revive peace talks**  
   Following recent diplomatic efforts including the Saudi defense minister's trip to Iran and reports of potential post-midterm U.S. military action, Vice President Vance engaged Iran to revive peace talks.

5. **UK Chancellor John Healey raises bank taxes in Autumn Budget**  
   Following anticipation over fiscal discipline in the Burnham government's first Autumn Budget and a newly proposed package to cushion household energy bills, UK Chancellor John Healey raised taxes on UK banks and vowed to keep public finances stable.
